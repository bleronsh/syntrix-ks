03 / Approach

We work the way
we'd want to be worked with.

We write it down first. Before code, a short document: what the system is for, who it serves, what it must not do. The page is cheap; the bug after launch isn't. A page of writing trades a week of meetings for an afternoon of thinking, and almost always replaces a quarter of disagreement with a paragraph everyone has already agreed to.

We build the boring parts well. Logging, backups, error states, the second-Tuesday-of-the-month edge case — the unloved scaffolding that, when it works, disappears from the conversation entirely. Boring done well is what people quietly rely on for years, and is the closest thing our trade has to compound interest.

We stay close, then leave clean. We work alongside your team, share what we learn, and hand over systems someone else can confidently inherit. No vendor lock, no theatre, no — so, about the renewal. The mark of good work is that the next person doesn't need us to keep it running.

We say no more than yes. We turn down work that doesn't fit, scope features that don't earn their place, and occasionally tell you the right answer is doing less than you asked for. The cost of a small honest conversation now is almost always smaller than the bill for building the wrong thing carefully.
